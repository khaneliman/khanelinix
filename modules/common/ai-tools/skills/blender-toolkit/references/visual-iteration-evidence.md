# Historical visual-iteration evidence

These observations explain the lessons in
[visual iteration](visual-iteration.md). Read them when investigating a
measurement limit, registration error, or failed acceptance method. Dimensions
and counts describe those sessions only, not calibrated tolerances or universal
edit sizes for another asset.

## Inspection and reference limits

Soft-lit skin under AgX let a featureless paddle pass as a hand through several
reviews. A fixed multi-angle Workbench clay sheet exposes form that presentation
lighting can hide; the main guide records the render settings.

A head about 50 px tall on a 16-figure board could not show sub-millimetre
changes. A single-subject sheet raised it to about 400 px at the same image
size. One image model drew most profiles facing one way until each got its own
sheet with the facing in the prompt. A generated profile also put the ear top
about 12 mm above the front view, requiring a choice of which view to follow.

## Registration and inherited assumptions

Moving an estimated crown under hair shifted a measured eye-height gap from 15.5
mm to about 5 mm. These registration errors also reversed verdicts:

- A crop cutting the head or figure pinned its silhouette to the crop edge.
- An illustrated profile drew the cornea, one eyeball radius ahead of the
  eyeball centre; registering on the centre misaligned the comparison.
- A bald model against a haired outline misrepresented skull size until a closed
  hair block-in existed or the reference showed the hairline.
- Sizing heads by figure height blamed the head for a body proportion.
- A front render's apparent face edge was a shading terminator.

Crops, hair, and misattributed feature ownership repeatedly made defects
inherited from notes, packets, or earlier rounds incorrect. Looking or measuring
was necessary before treating those premises as current faults.

## Correction size and visual judgment

The head pass the user judged a clear improvement changed hair mass, then nose,
ear, and jaw placement, then eye size, spacing, iris, lids, and brows.
Expression remained open. That pass moved whole features 5 to 30 mm at a time
after four rounds of sub-millimetre falloff nudges had passed every self-check
without resemblance. The useful distinction was whole-feature proportion versus
local relief, not a fixed millimetre target.

Across four heads, 23 whole-face checks never vetoed a step while the user saw
no resemblance. Comparing the whole subject still helps detect drift into
caricature from per-fault "improved" verdicts, but it did not establish
likeness. The user also redirected a variant round to nose, ear, and neck faults
visible at a glance. Variants were not a substitute for fixing those obvious
mismatches.

## Measurement failures

A ratio exit criterion turned a cast into scaled copies of one face. Minimizing
a silhouette-width error gave a lumpier body that the user rejected on sight.
Per-row extents from the panel and a `film_transparent` render, normalized to
figure or head height, traced a "stocky toddler" appearance to arm standoff and
narrow trousers and feet. Outlines merged arms into width and could not show
whether a surface read as a body. These measurements located discrepancies; they
did not replace the image as the acceptance evidence.
